Stabilizer jack hand-crankNEW

The removable crank handle used to raise and lower a trailer's stabilizer jacks.

$250 deductible per occurrenceUp to $2,500 per coverage durationParked (stationary) only

What it is

Travel trailers and fifth wheels have scissor-style stabilizer jacks at the corners that are lowered at camp to reduce bounce and sway. The hand-crank is the removable L-shaped handle that turns the jack screw to raise and lower them. It is easy to leave behind at a campsite.

Typical parts cost

Replacement crank handles commonly run about $10 to $30.

Photo examples

What the Shield covers

  • A missing or broken stabilizer jack hand-crank

Not covered

  • The stabilizer jacks themselves
  • Tongue jacks, power jacks, and leveling systems
  • Damage from driving with jacks down

How RV and trailer rentals usually handle this

In the RV and trailer rental industry, small interior items like this are usually treated as the renter's responsibility. Rental agreements commonly list them on a damage or replacement-fee schedule and deduct the cost from the security deposit, while the larger protection plans offered with rentals are built around major physical damage and often carry deductibles higher than the repair itself. A damage waiver such as the Stress Free Shield is designed to fill that gap.

Stabilizer jacks are for steadying only, not lifting or leveling. Damage from misusing them is usually a renter charge, and lost cranks are a common checkout item.

Avoid it: Use the jacks to steady the trailer only, never to lift or level it, and put the crank back in its compartment before you leave.
