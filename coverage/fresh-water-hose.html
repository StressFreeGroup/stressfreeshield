Fresh water hose and grommetNEW

The drinking-water hose that connects the RV to a water spigot, and its rubber washer.

$250 deductible per occurrenceUp to $2,500 per coverage durationParked (stationary) only

What it is

The fresh water hose is a drinking-water-safe hose, usually white or blue, that connects a campground spigot to the RV's city water inlet. The grommet is the small rubber washer inside each hose fitting that creates the seal; when it is missing or torn, the connection leaks.

Typical parts cost

Drinking-water hoses commonly run about $20 to $50. Grommets cost a few dollars.

Photo examples

What the Shield covers

  • The fresh (drinking) water hose
  • Hose grommets (rubber washers) in the fittings

Not covered

  • Water damage of any kind
  • The RV water pump, lines, tanks, or water heater
  • Using the fresh water hose for anything other than drinking water

How RV and trailer rentals usually handle this

In the RV and trailer rental industry, small interior items like this are usually treated as the renter's responsibility. Rental agreements commonly list them on a damage or replacement-fee schedule and deduct the cost from the security deposit, while the larger protection plans offered with rentals are built around major physical damage and often carry deductibles higher than the repair itself. A damage waiver such as the Stress Free Shield is designed to fill that gap.

Rental units typically include a dedicated drinking-water hose, and lost or damaged hoses are a common checkout charge.

Avoid it: Use the pressure regulator if the unit has one, coil the hose when you leave, and never use it to rinse the sewer hose.
